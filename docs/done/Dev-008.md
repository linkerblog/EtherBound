# Dev-008: Isometric renderer with grass sprite (Web)

[Feature] [Rendering] [Input] [Architecture]

The camera moves from top-down 3/4 to isometric. This was decided with the user on 22/09/2026, and
`docs/utils/VISION.md` [Sec. 1], [Sec. 3] and [Sec. 5] already say so. LimeZu is dropped as
runtime art: the user draws their own sprites and uses the packs only as a base. At the user's
request, the new four-frame grass tile sheet is used for grass tops; other materials retain flat
colour vector placeholders with shaded faces. The broader art pipeline remains later work.

The change is client-only. The server, the protocol and the generated schema stay as they are:
the world model (1 m tiles, `h` in half-metres, walls on the N/W edges of their tile) already
matches an isometric view. N and W are exactly the back edges of an isometric tile.

Reference: `docs/utils/VISION.md` [Sec. 3], [Sec. 5]; `docs/utils/STYLEGUIDE.md` [Sec. 1];
`docs/done/Dev-004.md` (integer zoom, radius-2 streaming); `docs/done/Fix03.md` (timed steps);
`CONTEXT.md`.

**Depends on** `docs/Fix04.md`, which changes `MapScene.update`'s step loop. Land it first. Keep
the uncommitted right-click fix in `MapScene.ts`, which adds the `canvasBounds` offsets to
`screenX`/`screenY`.

## 1. Decisions to approve

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Projection | 2:1 pixel isometric with a fixed camera and no rotation. World +x runs screen right-down, world +y runs screen left-down | The N and W edges of a tile are its back edges, so a tile's own walls never hide the body standing on it (the Zomboid convention, which the world model already follows) |
| Scale | A 1 m tile is a 64×32 px diamond. One `h` unit (0.5 m) is 16 px, which is half the tile height | One `h` unit moves the screen by exactly one diagonal row, so heights and rows share one integer grid. That keeps sorting and picking exact, and the view angle is true isometric (35.26°). A diamond covers 1024 px², the same as the old 32×32 square, so zoom x1–x4 and Dev-004's radius-2 streaming limit still hold. A 2 m body is 64 px and a 3 m floor is 96 px, a common base for hand-made sprites |
| Placeholders | Four-frame grass atlas for grass tops; vector `Graphics` for other material tops, darker side faces, and wall quads | The camera gets validated with the available grass sprite while retaining placeholders for other materials |
| Draw order | A painter's order by diagonal row (`x + y`), split at Niko's feet: everything below the plane `h = viewerH` goes to one base layer per chunk, and everything above it goes to per-row layers that sort with Niko. Vertical faces and walls that cross the plane are cut into two quads | Whatever lies above the plane always hides what lies below it, and nothing below the plane can hide Niko. This removes feet clipping and the "wall bottom pokes over a step" error that pure row sorting has |
| Cutaway | **Roofed rule.** Niko is *roofed* when the ground of his own tile is above his feet (he is underground), or when a floor slab lies above him in his tile or in a neighbour tile that is not walled off from his. When roofed, floors, walls and in-building ground more than 2 m above him are hidden. When not roofed, nothing is hidden. Updates VISION [Sec. 5] | Today's rule hides everything above `viewerH + 4` everywhere. In isometric view that would show every building from outside as its lowest band. The neighbour check stops the whole building from flashing back while Niko is on a stair tile with an open stairwell above |
| Front walls | Walls on Niko's floor that stand in front of him, inside a window around him, are drawn as a 0.5 m stub. Updates VISION [Sec. 5] | Otherwise a 3 m wall hides him whenever he walks behind it (The Sims / Zomboid cutaway) |
| Movement keys | Screen-relative: W is screen up, which is world (−1, −1)/√2. W+D is world north. The client sends the normalized world vector | This is the standard for isometric games. `InputMessage` already accepts float `dx`/`dy` in [−1, 1], so the protocol does not change. Walking into a wall slides along it at 4 × 0.707 = 2.83 m/s, which the server and the prediction already do per axis |
| Scope | Web only (`web.game`, `web.world`). The server, protocol and schema stay untouched | The server does not know about the camera |

## 2. Projection

`x`, `y` are world metres (continuous); `h` is half-metres.

```text
sx = (x - y) * 32
sy = (x + y) * 16 - h * 16
```

- Tile `(tx, ty)` at height `h` has four corners: top `(tx, ty)`, right `(tx+1, ty)`, bottom
  `(tx+1, ty+1)` and left `(tx, ty+1)`. The N edge runs top→right and the W edge runs top→left.
  Both are back edges.
- The visible side faces of a column face south (edge left→bottom) and east (edge bottom→right).
- **Inverse ray:** `s = sx / 32` and `t = sy / 16`. At height `h` the screen point is world
  `x = (s + t + h) / 2`, `y = (t + h - s) / 2`.
- **Keys to world:** the screen direction `(kx, ky)` (right − left, down − up) maps to world
  `(kx + ky, ky - kx)`, normalized. W → (−0.7071, −0.7071); W+D → (0, −1).

## 3. What gets built

### 3.1 `web/src/game/iso.ts` (new, `web.game`)
- Constants: `TILE_W = 64`, `TILE_H = 32`, `H_PX = 16`.
- `toScreen(x, y, h): { sx, sy }`, `screenToRay(sx, sy): { s, t }` and
  `keysToWorld(kx, ky): Direction` (normalized; `{0, 0}` when no key is held).
- Depth helpers: `BASE_DEPTH = -1_000_000`, `baseDepth(cx, cy) = BASE_DEPTH + cx + cy`,
  `rowDepth(row) = 2 * row`, and `nikoDepth(x, y) = 2 * (floor(x) + floor(y)) + 1`.
- `TILE_SIZE` and `CLIFF_H` are removed from `MapScene.ts`.

### 3.2 Layers (`MapScene.ts`)
- `ChunkLayers` becomes `{ base: Graphics; rows: Map<number, Graphics>; grass: Image[]; bbox }`.
  - `base` has depth `baseDepth(cx, cy)`.
  - Each `rows` entry is keyed by the global row `tx + ty`, has depth `rowDepth(row)`, and is
    created lazily. Grass tile images share the corresponding base/row depth and are only created
    inside the expanded camera view; their frame choice is stable from world coordinates.
  - On redraw, clear `base` and every row, then destroy the rows that stayed empty.
- One `drawChunk(chunk)` draws the terrain and the levels together, replacing
  `drawChunk`/`drawChunkLevels`. Walk the rows in ascending order (`b = 0 … 2·size − 2`,
  `lx = max(0, b − size + 1) … min(b, size − 1)`, `ly = b − lx`). Per tile, draw in this order:
  1. The ground top.
  2. The ground's south face, then its east face.
  3. Each level by ascending `z`: the floor top, the slab faces, the N wall, then the W wall.
- **Split rule:** a top face with `h ≤ viewerH` goes to `base`, and one above goes to its row.
  A vertical quad from `h0` to `h1` puts `[h0, min(h1, viewerH)]` in `base` and
  `[max(h0, viewerH), h1]` in its row, skipping the empty parts.
- Draw with `fillTriangle` only: two triangles per diamond or quad.
- Colours: tops use today's `shade(materialColor, h)`. South-facing quads (south faces, N walls)
  are the top colour × 0.82, and east-facing quads (east faces, W walls) are × 0.66. Scale each
  channel.

### 3.3 Terrain
- South face: drawn when `h > hS`, where `hS` is the south neighbour's ground, read through
  `ChunkStore.groundH` across chunk borders as today. It spans `hS → h`. The east face works the
  same with `hE`.
- An unknown neighbour (the world edge) gets a skirt down to `h − 4`.
- Any difference of 1 or more gets a face, so a 0.5 m step is 16 px, a climb 32–48 px, and a
  cliff taller than that.
- **Ground cutaway:** in a tile that holds any level cell with `floor_h ≠ -32768`, the ground top
  and its faces follow the cutaway [Sec. 3.4]. Anywhere else the ground is always drawn. The
  test building's interior ground (`h = 12`) is its ground floor, and without this rule it would
  cover Niko in the basement.

### 3.4 Levels, cutaway, front walls
- New file `web/src/world/cutaway.ts` (`web.world`):
  - `cutoffH(store, x, y, viewerH): number` returns `viewerH + 4` when roofed and `Infinity`
    otherwise.
  - **Roofed** means:
    - `groundH(own tile) > viewerH`, or
    - some tile in the 3×3 around Niko has a level cell with `floor_h ≠ -32768` and
      `floor_h > viewerH`, and that tile is Niko's own or not walled off from it. An orthogonal
      neighbour is walled off when `wallBetween(store, own, neighbour, viewerH)` is true. A
      diagonal neighbour counts when at least one of its two L paths has no wall on either leg.
      Doorways do not wall off, windows do (the same as `rules.wallBetween`).
  - VOID flags are ignored here, as the renderer ignores them today.
- Floors are drawn when `floor_h ≤ cutoff`, skipping VOID cells.
  - The top goes at `floor_h`.
  - Slab faces are 1 unit thick (`floor_h − 1 → floor_h`), on the south and east sides where the
    neighbour cell in the same level has no floor at the same `floor_h`.
- **Wall base:** `floor_h`, or the ground height / nearest lower slab for a floorless cell, using
  the shared `wallBaseH` rule. Fix01 removed the `z * 6` fallback. Walls are drawn when
  `base ≤ cutoff`. **Floorless walls get this check too**: every perimeter wall of the test
  building sits in a floorless cell.
- **Wall quads:** an N wall stands on the N edge and a W wall on the W edge, 6 units tall, with a
  1 px lighter top line.
  - A doorway draws nothing (as today).
  - A window draws solid quads for `base → base+2` and `base+4 → base+6`, with glass in between:
    `0xd8e6f0` at α 0.45.
- **Front-wall stub**, with Niko's tile `(nx, ny)`, midpoint `(mx, my)` of the edge, and
  constants `CUT_DEPTH = 8` and `CUT_WIDTH = 3`. A wall is drawn 1 unit tall, windows included,
  when all of these hold:
  1. It is in front: an N edge of `(wx, wy)` needs `wy > ny`, and a W edge needs `wx > nx`.
  2. `0 < (mx + my) − (nx + ny + 1) ≤ CUT_DEPTH`.
  3. `|(mx − my) − (nx − ny)| ≤ CUT_WIDTH`.
  4. It spans Niko's height: `base < viewerH + 4` and `base + 6 > viewerH`.

  The rule only uses Niko's tile, so it changes only when he crosses a tile.

### 3.5 Niko
- The circle `Arc` becomes a `Graphics` drawn once in local coordinates:
  - A shadow: `fillEllipse(0, 0, 27, 13)`, black at α 0.35.
  - A body: `fillRoundedRect(-9, -60, 18, 58, 6)` in `0x2583ff`, with a 2 px white stroke.
- Every frame: position `toScreen(x, y, h ?? viewerH)` and depth `nikoDepth(x, y)`. The camera
  keeps following it.

### 3.6 Keys
- `readDirection()` returns `keysToWorld(right − left, down − up)`. Everything downstream is
  unchanged: `changed` detection, `flushPartialStep`, `sendMovementStep` and `render`. The eight
  possible vectors are fixed floats, so equality still detects a change.
- Telemetry stays in world metres.

### 3.7 Right-click picking
- New file `web/src/world/pick.ts` (`web.world`) with
  `pickTile(store, s, t, viewerH, cutoff): { x, y, z } | null`.
  - March `hs` from `viewerH + 40` down to `viewerH − 40` in steps of 0.5. At each sample take
    `x`, `y` from [Sec. 2], and the tile `floor(x)`, `floor(y)`.
  - A hit is a level cell with `floor_h ≠ -32768`, `floor_h ≤ cutoff` and
    `hs ≤ floor_h < hs + 0.5`, which returns `z = floor(floor_h / 6)`.
  - The ground is a hit when `hs ≤ groundH` and it is shown under the ground cutaway
    [Sec. 3.3]. It returns `z = floor(groundH / 6)`. The ground is a solid column, so clicking a
    cliff face picks the higher tile that owns it.
  - Walls are not pickable: menus target tiles, and a click on a wall picks what lies behind it.
- In `MapScene`: `screenToRay(pointer.worldX, pointer.worldY)`, then `pickTile`. With no hit, no
  menu opens. `screenX`/`screenY` keep the `canvasBounds` offsets.
- `ChunkStore.topmostZ` is removed, since it has no other caller.

### 3.8 Camera bounds and culling
- **Bounds:** the bounding box of the world diamond. With inclusive bounds
  `[minX, minY, maxX, maxY]`:
  - `sx` runs from `(minX − maxY − 1)·32` to `(maxX + 1 − minY)·32`.
  - `sy` runs from `(minX + minY)·16 − 32·16` to `(maxX + maxY + 2)·16 + 16·16`.

  The margins are 16 m of height above and 8 m below. The corners outside the diamond show the
  background.
- **Culling:** `drawChunk` stores the chunk's screen bbox, using `hMax` (max of ground and drawn
  levels, plus 6 for walls) and `hMin` (min ground, minus 4). Each frame, a chunk whose bbox
  does not meet `camera.worldView` grown by 64 px gets `setVisible(false)` on `base` and every
  row and grass sprites. Phaser redraws `Graphics` from its command list every frame, so this is
  the main cost cap.

### 3.9 Redraw triggers
| Trigger | Redraw |
|---|---|
| A chunk arrives or changes | That chunk and its 4 neighbours (as today; faces cross borders) |
| Materials load | Every chunk (as today) |
| `viewerH` or `cutoff` changes | Loaded chunks intersecting the camera view; offscreen chunks are deferred until they enter view |
| Niko's tile changes | Visible chunks redraw for front-wall stubs; offscreen chunks redraw when they enter view |
| The expanded camera view moves or resizes | Redraw intersecting chunks to materialize only on-screen grass sprites |
| Snapshot | Destroy every layer (as today) |

## 4. Traps

- **Floorless walls.** They are drawn with no cutaway today, which is invisible in top-down.
  In isometric view all four storeys of the test building's walls would show at once.
- **Ground under buildings.** The interior ground is the ground floor (`h = 12`). Without the
  ground cutaway it covers the basement.
- **Row sorting is not enough.** A wall standing on a lower step right behind a 1 m step would
  draw over that step, and Niko's feet get clipped by the row in front. The split plane fixes
  both, so do not "simplify" it away.
- **Niko's row comes from his centre tile, not his footprint.** The server lets a body pushing a
  wall get within about 0.05 m of it: `movement.py` snaps back to 0.3 m only when a crossing is
  attempted, and the prediction has no body radius at all. So the footprint can overlap the tile
  in front. The split plane keeps the feet visible there.
- **Equal depths.** Rows of the same number in different chunks never overlap on screen, so their
  order does not matter. Niko must use `2·row + 1`, never the row's own depth.
- **`fillPoints` and `fillPath` triangulate (earcut) on every frame.** Use `fillTriangle`.
- **Height pops.** The prediction sets `h` when Niko crosses a tile, so on ramps he jumps 16 px
  per step. This is expected with terraced terrain.
- **Streaming at x1 on a 4K screen.** The view reaches about 64 m from Niko along `x`/`y`, which
  is the same limit Dev-004 measured. Raised terrain shows a few rows more at the top.
- **Versions.** `Fix04.md` still lists `web.game v0.0.9 → v0.0.10`, but the working tree already
  has `v0.1.0`. Bump from whatever is current when this lands.

## 7. Checks and acceptance

Checks: `cd web && npm run gen:types && git diff --exit-code src/net/schema.d.ts && npm run build`.
Also run the server checks, which must pass unchanged since no server file changes.

Manual, with `EtherBound.exe`, on a new game with seed 0 (test world):
1. **Spawn** on the road: diamonds, with side faces wherever the height changes. Grass tiles use
   the new atlas; a 0.5 m step, a 1 m rise and a cliff are distinguishable.
2. **Keys:** W moves up the screen, and W+D moves world north (`POS` Y decreases, X stays).
   `SPD` reads 4.00 on grass for single keys and for key pairs. Releasing causes no jump
   (Fix03 acceptance 1).
3. **Terrace ramp** (x 108–114, y ≈ 89–92): Niko rises 16 px per tile.
4. **Right-click** a flat tile, a cliff face (the menu is for the higher tile), and a tile just
   behind a raised terrace. `inspect` names the tile under the cursor. Right-clicking empty
   background opens no menu.
5. **Building** (x 136–160, y 132–160) from outside: the whole building is visible, roof
   included. On its north-west side the walls on Niko's band in front of him are stubs.
6. **West doorway** (136, 146): stepping into it hides the upper floors and the roof. The front
   walls are stubs, and the back walls stand full height with windows.
7. **Stairs** to the first floor and the roof: the building does not flash while Niko is on the
   stairs. On the roof everything is visible.
8. **Basement** by the south flight (x 145–150, y 152): the ground-floor lid is hidden and the
   basement walls are visible.
9. **Park pit** (x 48–72, y 176–200), entered by its east ramp: the pit's north and west walls
   show as faces.
10. **Dig** on grass: after the chunk update, the tile drops and its neighbours' faces appear.
11. **Performance** at x1, 1920×1080, walking across chunk borders and up and down ramps:
    ≥ 55 fps, and no frame over 50 ms when the height changes (DevTools Performance).
12. **Zoom** x1–x4 stays crisp, and the camera stops at the world diamond's bounding box.

## TL;DR

The camera goes isometric: 2:1, fixed, 64×32 px per 1 m tile, 16 px per 0.5 m of height. Only the
web client changes; the server, the protocol and the world model stay as they are. Grass uses the
new four-frame tile atlas; vector placeholders remain for other materials.

The renderer:
- **Sorts** by diagonal row, split at Niko's feet, so nothing below him can hide him and nothing
  above him is drawn under what lies below it.
- **Draws every height step as a face**, so steps, climbs and cliffs read differently.
- **Hides upper floors only when Niko is roofed**, and cuts the walls in front of him to stubs.
- **Picks** the right-click tile with a height-aware ray.
- **Culls** off-screen chunks.

WASD becomes screen-relative with no protocol change. `web.game` and `web.world` get one bump
each. Fix04 lands first.
