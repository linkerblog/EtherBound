# Dev-002: World structure (Phase 1)

[World] [Engine] [Persistence] [Networking]

The first Phase 1 doc. It replaces the throwaway `testmap.py` with the real world model: chunks,
a fine heightmap, building floors, an excavable underground and materials with properties. Niko
walks over it through the same `move` verb, climbing slopes and stairs. No NPCs, no verbs other
than `move`, no event bus yet; they all need this layer first.

Design reference: `utils/VISION.md` [Sec. 5] (world model), [Sec. 6] primitives 1 and 2, [Sec. 15]
Phase 1.

## 1. Decisions to approve

These are the concrete choices behind [Sec. 5] of the vision. On approval they are written into
`utils/VISION.md` [Sec. 5] before any code [Sec. 9].

| Topic | Decision | Why (real life / cost) |
|---|---|---|
| Tile and chunk | 1 m tile, 32×32-tile chunks (32 m) | Small enough to stream, large enough to keep row counts low |
| Elevation unit | Integer **half-metres** (`h`). `h = 3` is 1.5 m | The 0.5 m step the vision asks for, with no float drift |
| Levels | `z` is an **absolute** 3 m band: `z = floor(h / 6)` | A tunnel under a hill is horizontal in reality; relative levels would bend it |
| Standing rule | Every walkable spot has a standing elevation. A body may step to a neighbouring tile if the height difference is at most 0.5 m, it has 2 m of headroom and no wall stands on the shared edge | One rule covers slopes, kerbs, ramps, stairs and walls |
| Stairs | Graded tiles (0.5 m rise per tile, 6 tiles per floor), not a special link | No special case; they fall out of the standing rule |
| Vertical links | Only for jumps above 0.5 m: ladders (`climbable` flag) now, lifts later | These are the only cases the standing rule cannot express |
| Underground | Implicit: below the ground surface everything is solid, material given by per-chunk strata. Only changes are stored (voids, basements) | "Only existing levels are stored"; an untouched hill costs nothing |
| Digging an open hole | Lowers the surface heightfield | A pit is simply lower ground |
| Walls | **Edges**: a wall sits on the border between two tiles, never fills a tile. Each tile owns its north and west edge; its south and east edges belong to its neighbours | A real wall is about 0.2 m thick; full-tile walls would waste a metre per wall and make rooms a tile smaller on each side |
| Openings | A doorway or window is a kind of wall edge: a doorway lets bodies through, a window lets sight through but not bodies | Doors and windows are gaps in walls, not tiles |
| Art | Placeholder colours per material + height shading + cliff lines. LimeZu comes in its own doc | Autotiling LimeZu is a separate job and must not block the model. LimeZu draws walls as full tiles, so the art doc must adapt its wall sprites to edges |
| World content | A seeded **test world** [Sec. 4.4], not the city | City authoring is still open in `PENDING.md`; the model supports both procedural and imported data |

## 2. Data model

### 2.1 Materials (primitive 1)
- Static definitions in `server/src/etherbound/world/materials.toml`: `key`, `name`, `color`
  (placeholder), `walkable`, `walk_cost`, `solid`, `blocks_sight`, `diggable`, `dig_cost`,
  `flammable`, `density`, `resistance`, `liquid`, `tags`.
- Starting set: grass, dirt, topsoil, clay, rock, sand, asphalt, concrete, brick, wood_floor,
  tile_floor, roofing, water_shallow, water_deep, glass.
- Chunk blobs store small integer ids. The `material` table maps `key → id`, **append-only**: a
  new key gets the next id and existing ids never change, so reordering the TOML never corrupts a
  save.
- `resistance`, `flammable` and `density` are stored but unused until physics and fire exist.

### 2.2 Chunk (surface)
Table `chunk`, pk `(cx, cy)`:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| `ground_h` | blob, 32×32 `int16`: ground surface elevation |
| `surface_mat` | blob, 32×32 `uint16`: material on top of the ground |
| `strata` | JSON: `[(depth_h, material_key), ...]` from the surface down |
| `revision` | int, bumped on every edit; the client re-renders on change |
| `gen_version` | int, generator version that produced it |

Strata are stored per chunk, not recomputed, so a newer generator never silently changes an
existing world underground.

### 2.3 Chunk level (floors, basements, voids)
Table `chunk_level`, pk `(cx, cy, z)`, one row only where something exists at that band:

| Blob (32×32) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `floor_h` `int16` | standing elevation of the floor slab, sentinel = no floor |
| `floor_mat` `uint16` | floor material |
| `wall_n` `uint16` | material of the wall on the tile's north edge, 0 = none |
| `wall_w` `uint16` | material of the wall on the tile's west edge, 0 = none |
| `edge_flags` `uint8` | per edge (north, west): `doorway`, `window` |
| `flags` `uint8` | `void` (excavated, overrides implicit ground), `climbable`, `roof` |

A wall belongs to the level it is stored in and spans that level's floor to the next 3 m
(`floor_h` to `floor_h + 6`). The east edge of a chunk's last column is the west edge of the
neighbouring chunk's first column, so walls on chunk borders need no special case.

A building is only cells and edges: floors, walls, doorways, windows and stair tiles. Its roof is
a floor slab flagged `roof` (walkable if you can reach it, as in reality). A basement is a set of
`void` cells with a floor below the ground.

### 2.4 Solidity and standing
Two questions, answered separately:
- **Volume** ("what is at `(x, y, h)`"): below `ground_h` and not inside a `void` cell → solid,
  material from strata. Otherwise open. Walls never fill a volume.
- **Edge** ("can a body cross from tile A to tile B at `h`"): blocked if the shared edge has a
  wall (other than a doorway) in any level whose span overlaps `h` to `h + 4` (the body's 2 m).

The **standing surfaces** of a tile are the ground (unless excavated) plus every floor slab in
that column. Moving picks the one within 0.5 m of the body's current `h` with 2 m (4 `h`) of
open headroom, and only if the edge between the two tiles can be crossed.

### 2.5 Actor
- New column `h` (int). `z` stays and is derived by the engine on every commit (`h // 6`).
- Spawn comes from the generator (on the road), no longer the hardcoded `(2, 2)`.

## 3. Movement and navigation

- `move_on_map` becomes `move_in_world`: the same sub-step loop with the `r = 0.3` body circle,
  but each tile the circle enters must pass the standing rule from the body's current `h`. On
  success the body takes the new tile's standing `h` (and `z`).
- Walls collide as segments on tile edges with 0.1 m of thickness on each side, so Niko stops
  against the wall line, not at the tile centre. Doorways are gaps in that segment.
- **Slope cost:** speed ×0.6 when stepping up 0.5 m, ×0.85 stepping down, times the material's
  `walk_cost` (mud, sand and shallow water are slower). Energy cost waits for needs.
- Drops above 0.5 m are refused for walking; falling and jumping come with physics.
- `water_deep` is not walkable (swimming is a later verb).
- **Navigation** (`world/nav.py`): A* over standing spots `(x, y, h)` with 8-neighbour moves
  following the same rule, plus ladder edges. A diagonal move is allowed only if both orthogonal
  paths around it are open, so paths never cut the corner of a wall. The player never uses it;
  NPCs will. It lives here because it is the proof that the world is traversable as data.
- All movement checks read the in-memory chunk cache. No database reads in the 20 Hz loop.

## 4. Server

### 4.1 `server/src/etherbound/world/` (new module)
| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `materials.py` + `materials.toml` | Registry, TOML load, append-only id mapping |
| `chunk.py` | `Chunk`, `ChunkLevel`, blob encode/decode with `array` (no numpy) |
| `grid.py` | `WorldGrid`: chunk cache, `solid_at`, `wall_between`, `standing_surfaces`, `can_step`, bounds |
| `nav.py` | A* over standing spots |
| `gen/noise.py` | Seeded value noise; its permutation comes from the `worldgen` RNG stream |
| `gen/testworld.py` | The test world generator [Sec. 4.4] |

### 4.2 Engine
- `WorldEngine` owns a `WorldGrid` instead of `TestMap`. It stays the only writer: the generator
  *returns* chunk data, the engine commits it to the database and the cache.
- `ensure_world` generates the world if the save has no chunks (this also upgrades an existing
  Phase 0 save), and moves Niko to spawn if his stored position no longer stands on anything.
- `new_game` wipes actors, chunks and levels through the ORM and regenerates from the new seed.
- Lazy chunk loading is the API shape (`grid.chunk(cx, cy)` loads or generates and commits), but
  the test world is generated eagerly on new game.

### 4.3 Persistence
- Migration `0002_world`: tables `material`, `chunk`, `chunk_level`; column `actor.h` (default 0).
- `world_meta` gains `gen_version`.

### 4.4 Test world (`gen_version = 1`)
Fixture content that exercises every rule. 8×8 chunks (256 × 256 m):
- Rolling hills from value noise, up to about 6 m, flatter to the south.
- A 1.5 m terrace (cliff) crossed by a ramp.
- An asphalt road, levelled, with the spawn point.
- One building on a slope: levelled base, ground floor + first floor + a basement exposed on the
  downhill side, brick edge walls with doorways and windows, internal stairs, roof slab. One
  outer wall runs along a chunk border.
- A pond: shallow ring, deep centre.
- A 2 m pit with a ramp in the park (a lowered heightfield).

### 4.5 Network and routes
- **WS `chunk`** (server → client): `cx, cy, revision, ground_h[], surface_mat[]` and a
  `levels[]` list of `{z, floor_h[], floor_mat[], wall_n[], wall_w[], edge_flags[], flags[]}`. Plain integer arrays
  (about 1024 numbers each) keep them typed through Pydantic; a binary encoding can come later if
  measurements ask for it.
- The hub pushes chunks, the client does not request them: on connect, and whenever Niko enters a
  new chunk, it sends every chunk within radius 2 (5×5) that this connection does not have at its
  current revision.
- `snapshot` gains `world: {chunk_size, level_h, bounds}`; `ActorSnapshot` and `ack` gain `h`.
- `GET /api/materials`: the registry for rendering and tooltips.
- `GET /api/world/chunk?cx&cy`: the same payload over REST, for debugging.
- `GET /api/menu` accepts any `z` and adds `target` ("grass · 1.5 m"). Verbs stay `["inspect"]`;
  real verbs are a later doc.

## 5. Client

### 5.1 `web/src/world/` (new module)
- `ChunkStore`: keeps received chunks, replaces them on a new revision.
- `rules.ts`: a small mirror of the standing rule, edge walls and slope cost, used by prediction. The server
  always wins.
- `materials.ts`: fetches `/api/materials` once.

### 5.2 Rendering (placeholder)
- One render texture per chunk, redrawn when its revision changes.
- Colour by material, darker with lower `h`; a light edge line wherever neighbouring tiles differ
  by 1 m or more (cliffs); walls as thick lines on their edges, with a darker strip on
  east-west walls to suggest 3/4 depth; doorways as gaps; windows as a lighter dashed line.
- **Cutaway:** floor slabs and walls more than 2 m above Niko are hidden, so inside a building you
  see your own floor, and outside you see roofs.
- No vertical screen offset by height yet; that belongs with the LimeZu art pass.

### 5.3 Input
- Prediction uses `rules.ts` against the `ChunkStore` so Niko no longer walks through walls and
  snaps back.
- Right-click picks the topmost visible surface in the tile and sends its `z`; the menu shows the
  server's `target` line (`utils/STYLEGUIDE.md` [Sec. 8] `.target`).
- Camera bounds come from `snapshot.world.bounds`.

## 7. What must not break

- The engine is the only writer: the generator proposes chunks, the engine commits them.
- One action API: climbing stairs and slopes is still `MoveAction`; there is no `climb` shortcut
  for the player.
- Determinism: generation uses only the `worldgen` stream; the same seed gives identical chunk
  bytes.
- An existing Phase 0 save opens after `0002_world` without a wipe: the world is generated and
  Niko is placed on it.
- Dev-001 acceptance still holds: WASD without visible lag, pause freezes Niko, restart restores
  time and position (now with `h`), the menu comes from the server.
- The 20 Hz movement loop never touches the database for world lookups.
- TypeScript types stay generated.

## 8. Tests

- Generation: same seed → identical bytes; different seed → different; 8×8 chunks in under 1 s.
- Blobs: encode/decode round-trip.
- Materials: a new key is appended and existing ids stay the same.
- Standing rule: flat, +0.5 m allowed, +1 m blocked, low ceiling blocked, deep water blocked,
  excavated void walkable.
- Edges: a wall blocks crossing in both directions; a doorway lets it through; a window blocks
  it; a wall on a chunk border blocks from either chunk; a wall on the ground floor does not block
  the floor above; diagonal moves never cut a wall corner.
- Movement through `submit`: Niko climbs the test building's stairs and ends on the first floor
  (`h` and `z` updated); an uphill walk covers less distance than a flat one in the same time.
- Nav: a path from spawn to the first floor exists and uses the stairs; none through walls;
  unreachable returns `None`.
- Migration: `0001 → 0002` on a database with Phase 0 rows keeps the actor and gives it `h`.
- `new_game` wipes and regenerates.
- WS: connecting sends snapshot + 25 chunks; crossing a chunk boundary sends only the new ones.
- Menu: `target` names the tile's material and elevation.

## TL;DR

The world becomes real data: 32 m chunks with a half-metre heightmap, absolute 3 m levels,
buildings made of floor cells and edge walls (doorways and windows are edge kinds), an
underground that is implicit strata plus stored voids, and materials as an append-only registry.
One standing rule (step at most 0.5 m, 2 m headroom, no wall on the shared edge) covers slopes,
ramps, stairs and walls, so Niko climbs everything through the same `move` verb, and A* uses the
same rule for future NPCs. A seeded test world exercises every case, chunks
stream by radius over WS, the client renders placeholders with height shading and cutaway, and
`testmap.py` is deleted. Two new modules, seven bumped, one migration.
