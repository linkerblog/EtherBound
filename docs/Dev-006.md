# Dev-006: Fill and jump

[Feature] [Engine] [Physics] [World]

Two catalog ops with a physical core have no handler: `fill` and `jump`. `dig` can lower the ground
but nothing can put it back, and the only ways across a gap are a detour or `climb`, which handles a
1-1.5 m step and nothing else. This plan adds both as ordinary handlers on the existing action API:
`fill` is the counterpart of `dig`, and `jump` is an energy-limited standing jump resolved inside one
`submit`, like `push` and `throw`. No schema change, no new system, no new primitive.

## 1. Findings

Read from the source (`BodyOps.cs`, `PhysicsOps.cs`, `Movement.cs`, `WorldGrid.cs`, `Menu.cs`);
nothing here was run.

- **Dig leaves no trace of the removed earth.** `WorldGrid.LowerGround` does `ground_h -= 1`,
  `dug += 1` and sets `surface_mat` to the stratum at `dug + 1`. The removed half-metre is discarded:
  no spoil object, no mass. Strata depth counts from the original ground (`ground_h + dug`), so the
  data to undo a dig is already there: `dug` is exactly how many cells can be put back.
- **The original surface is not stored.** After the first dig the grass is gone from `surface_mat`;
  a refill can restore the stratum, not the grass.
- **Standing rule.** `move` steps at most 1 half-metre cell (`Δh <= 1`). `climb` takes 1 min to an
  orthogonal neighbour whose surface is 2-3 cells away and is offered only when the plain step is
  impossible. Nothing crosses a one-tile gap (pond, pit, void), and `climb` refuses a drop above
  1.5 m ("too high to climb").
- **Resolution style.** `PhysicsOp` resolves a whole path at submit time and returns a
  `Resolution` with a trajectory that `TrajectoryAnimator` plays (`WorldClient.cs:727`); falls over
  3 m emit `impact {target, energy}` through `Physics.Potential` and never touch health.
- **Constants.** `Physics` (`Movement.cs`) holds SI constants; `Movement.LoadMultiplier` and
  `ActionContext.LoadKg` already give the carried mass. Body mass is `ActorRow.MassKg` (80 kg default).
- **Menus.** `Menu.Build` offers an entry only if `Applies` holds, so a new op never adds noise
  unless a target qualifies. `jump` and `fill` already exist in `ops.toml` (`jump`: tile, `fill`: tile).
- **Extras.** `ExtrasBrain` only walks and waits; `Nav` never plans a jump.

## 2. Decisions

Marked **approval needed** where the choice is open.

- **D1. Fill restores, it does not conserve.** `fill` puts back one half-metre of earth on a tile
  with `dug > 0` and needs no item. **Approval needed.** The alternative, conserved earth (dig yields a
  `soil` object, fill consumes it), is the better Matter model but changes `dig` (a pile on the dug
  tile blocks the next dig, spoil needs mass, stacking and a place to go) and ties to "Recipes and
  supply" in `docs/PENDING.md`. Proposal: restore now, conserve in its own plan; D1 is the only
  decision that changes the work, so settle it before Stage 1.
- **D2. The refilled surface is the stratum, not the original.** A tile filled back to `dug = 0`
  shows the material the first dig removed (topsoil under grass), not grass: disturbed earth. Nothing
  regrows; vegetation is a later system. Settled by what is stored.
- **D3. Jump is energy-limited and mass-aware.** Take-off speed `JumpSpeed = 5.0` m/s at 45° for the
  bare body, so the effort is `E = ½·m_body·v²` and a carried load shares it: with
  `r = m_body / (m_body + load_kg)`, flat range `= JumpSpeed² / g · r` (2.55 m bare) and apex
  `= (JumpSpeed·sin 45°)² / (2g) · r` (0.64 m bare). Tile reach is `floor(range)` cardinal tiles and
  the rise allowed on landing is `floor(apex / 0.5)` half-metre cells. Values are a proposal,
  **approval needed** to calibrate; with them a bare 80 kg body clears 2 tiles and 1 cell, and
  roughly 22 kg of load takes both down a notch (one tile, no rise).
- **D4. Jump is offered only where walking cannot do the job.** Same discipline as `climb`: the
  landing tile must have a standing surface and (a) be one tile away with a drop above 3 cells
  (1.5 m, past `climb`), or (b) be two tiles away in a straight line with a middle tile the actor
  could not simply walk through (no standing surface within a step, or blocked). So menus stay clean
  and no entry duplicates `move`.
- **D5. Jump is instant, fill is durative.** `jump` resolves at submit (no activity, 0 game minutes,
  no stamina: there are no needs yet). `fill` is an activity with resumable progress, like `dig`.
- **D6. Physics still does not know health.** A drop above 3 m emits `impact` with potential energy;
  no HP, no injury. Jump never rolls anything; it is deterministic and replays from the journal.
- **D7. Cardinal only.** Four directions, like `push` and `throw`. Diagonal jumps wait for
  diagonal movement (see construction phase 3 in `docs/PENDING.md`).

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 `fill` (sim.world, sim.events, sim.engine)

- `WorldGrid.RaiseGround(x, y)`: the inverse of `LowerGround`: `ground_h += 1`, `dug -= 1`,
  `surface_mat = Stratum(dug_new + 1)`, the same formula `LowerGround` uses (so D2 follows from the
  data: at `dug = 0` it yields the first stratum, not the original surface). Returns the new chunk with `revision + 1`. Only the engine calls it.
- `FillOp` in `BodyOps.cs`, `Op = "fill"`, `RetainsWorkProgress = true`.
  - `Applies`: tile target on the ground surface whose `dug > 0`.
  - `Validate` (each refusal changes nothing): out of reach (same `InCloseReach` and `ReachH` as
    dig); "nothing to fill" (`dug == 0`); "something is on it" (object at the ground cell);
    "someone is standing there"; "no room above" (any floor, solid object or wall slot in the
    cells `ground+1..ground+4` of the raised tile, so the new surface keeps its headroom);
    "wall in the way" (a wall edge or interior slot on the tile, since `Edges.WallBottom` derives
    from the ground and would move the wall); "cannot restore" if the layer to restore is not walkable.
  - `Duration`: `ceil(MinutesPerCost * restored.DigCost / ToolFactor)`, the same formula and the
    same `tool.dig` factor as dig, so a shovel speeds both. Symmetry is deliberate.
  - `Complete`: raise the ground, raise whoever stands on the tile (the mirror of dig's "lowered"
    rule, `actor.moved` with reason `raised`), mark the chunk and emit `terrain.filled`
    `{tile, restored, dug}` and `chunk.changed`.
- Interruption keeps `activity_work` progress exactly like dig; partial work never mutates terrain.

### 3.2 `jump` (sim.engine)

- `Physics.JumpRange(bodyMass, loadKg)` and `Physics.JumpRise(bodyMass, loadKg)` plus the
  `JumpSpeed` constant, next to the other SI formulas in `Movement.cs`.
- `JumpOp` in `PhysicsOps.cs` (it shares `Edges`, `PhysicsLog` and the trajectory types),
  `Op = "jump"`, `ChangesLoad = false`.
  - `Applies` and `Builds`: the D4 rule; one `GameAction.On("jump", TileTarget)` per qualifying
    landing tile, found by scanning the four cardinal lines up to the reach.
  - `Validate`: landing in cardinal line, within `floor(range)`; landing has a standing surface and
    is at most `rise` cells above the take-off, any drop below; no wall on any crossed tile edge at
    the flight height; the middle tile of a two-tile jump has its body volume
    (`H_mid+1..H_mid+3`, `H_mid = max(h_start, h_land) + rise`) free of terrain, solid objects and
    walls, but it needs no floor under it; nobody stands on the landing tile or overlaps it (reuse the
    body-overlap rule of `Movement.CanMoveBody`). Reasons are short strings: "too far",
    "too high", "wall in the way", "no room to jump", "someone is there", "nothing to land on".
  - `Resolve`: place the actor on the landing tile centre (`x + 0.5`, `y + 0.5`, standing `h`), as
    `climb` does. A drop above 3 m adds `impact {actor, energy = Physics.Potential(mass, drop)}`.
    Emit `actor.moved` (reason `jump`) and return the trajectory `start -> landing` so
    `TrajectoryAnimator` plays it; the interrupted-activity rule of `Submit` applies unchanged.
- Nav, `ExtrasBrain` and A* are untouched: Extras do not jump (D4 keeps the menu honest, and an
  Extra planning jumps is a Minds change).

### 3.3 Events and client (sim.events, game.app)

- `SimEvent.TerrainFilled` (`terrain.filled`) in `Events/SimEvent.cs`, next to `TerrainDug`.
- `game/app/SimulationEventPresenter.cs`: `terrain.filled` -> `FILLED <MATERIAL>` (category `act`),
  next to `terrain.dug`. `actor.moved` with reason `jump` needs no new text; the existing feed
  line for movement stays.
- Menus, the radial menu and the build panel need no change: they render what the sim returns. The
  radial menu scans radius 1, so a two-tile jump is only offered from the right-click list.
- Chunk remeshing is already driven by the chunk revision; no renderer change is expected. Confirm
  by eye that a refilled tile and Niko's raise are drawn correctly (A8).

## 4. What must not break

- The engine is the only writer; the client never simulates. `fill` and `jump` go through
  `WorldEngine.Submit` like every op, and are journaled and replayed (`InputReplayRules`).
- Determinism: no RNG, no wall-clock time. The same seed and inputs produce the same events.
- `dig` is unchanged: same validation, same duration, same events, same `activity_work` keys.
- `climb`, `move`, `push`, `throw`, `hit`, `break` and the standing rule are untouched. A jump never
  lets an actor end inside a body, a wall or a solid cell.
- Physics does not know health. Impacts stay `impact` events.
- `terrain.dug` consumers (presenter, replication) keep working; the new event is additive.
- Save compatibility: no migration. `dug` already persists; a filled tile is a normal tile.
- Menu goldens: entries for existing ops must not move. New `fill`/`jump` entries may appear only
  where a tile qualifies; the golden diff is reviewed line by line (A9).
- The 1,000-Extra benchmark: neither op runs per tick, so it must not move.

## 5. Acceptance

- **A1.** `sim/EtherBound.Sim.Tests/FillRules.cs`: dig then fill on a grass tile returns
  `ground_h` and `dug` to the original and sets the surface to the first stratum (D2); two digs
  and one fill leave `dug = 1`; a tile with `dug = 0` is refused ("nothing to fill").
- **A2.** `FillRules.cs`: refusals for an object on the tile, someone standing there, a wall on the
  tile, a floor within headroom and an unreachable tile; each leaves the world and the event log
  unchanged but is still journaled.
- **A3.** `FillRules.cs`: a shovel shortens `fill` like `dig`; an interrupted fill resumes with its
  retained minutes and does not mutate terrain until it completes; the filler is raised with the
  ground and `actor.moved` carries `raised`.
- **A4.** `sim/EtherBound.Sim.Tests/JumpRules.cs`: across a one-tile gap (the lab `steps` bay or a
  dug pit) a bare body lands two tiles away; the same body carrying 30 kg is refused ("too far");
  `Physics.JumpRange`/`JumpRise` match the D3 numbers at 0, 10, 22 and 40 kg.
- **A5.** `JumpRules.cs`: refusals for a wall on a crossed edge, no room above the middle tile,
  an actor on the landing tile, a landing above the rise, and no standing surface.
- **A6.** `JumpRules.cs`: a drop of 4 m lands and emits `impact` with `Physics.Potential`; a drop of
  2 m lands with no impact; `jump` is not offered where a plain step or `climb` already works (D4).
- **A7.** `InputReplayRules.cs`: a journal with fills and jumps replays to the same state and the
  same event sequence.
- **A8.** `game.tests/SimulationEventPresenterTests.cs`: `terrain.filled` presents as `FILLED ...`.
  Visual: a `--shots` capture of a refilled pit and of a jump trajectory on an isolated `--database`;
  `manual`, because the render and the animation cannot be asserted by a test.
- **A9.** `MenuGoldens.cs`: goldens are regenerated only for added `fill`/`jump` entries;
  the diff shows no change to any other entry. `npm run check` passes, including `Architecture.cs`.

## TL;DR

Add the two missing physical ops. `fill` is dig's mirror: it restores one half-metre of earth to a
dug tile (the stratum comes back, grass does not), raises whoever stands there, and is a resumable
activity sped by the shovel; the earth is not conserved yet (D1, approval needed). `jump` is an
instant, energy-limited standing jump: a bare 80 kg body clears two tiles across a gap or drops past
`climb`'s limit, a loaded one clears less, and it is offered only where walking cannot do the job.
Both ride the existing action API, journal and event bus; there is no schema change, no client
rendering change and no health.
